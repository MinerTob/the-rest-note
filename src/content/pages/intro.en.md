Hello, I am MinerTob.

If you are reading this, you have probably been wandering around The Rest Note for a while.

There is not much of a route to follow here. You can read an article, listen to some music, touch the keys a few times, or do nothing at all.

As for who I am —

It probably starts with the name.

## MinerTob

The name MinerTob did not mean all that much at first.

In the beginning there was only Miner — a miner.

Then one day, looking at my computer keyboard, I got a small idea from Tab. I swapped out a single letter, and three characters that had never meant anything in particular came together:

Tob.

And so there was MinerTob.

Back then I certainly would not have guessed that a name left behind so casually would stay with me this long.

It has shown up in games, beside my avatars, in many corners of the internet; and in the end it followed me here, and became the name of the person who keeps The Rest Note.

The funny thing is that a lot of things seem to happen first, and only later, over a long stretch of time, do we slowly understand what they mean.

Tob was like that too.

Later I noticed that my life happens to contain two keyboards that matter a great deal to me.

One of them has eighty-eight black and white keys.

When my fingers come down on it, it does not ask me to solve anything.

The things left unfinished, the questions I have not figured out, and the voices in reality that sometimes wear me out — all of them can wait outside the piano bench for a while.

Sometimes I practise a piece seriously, correcting the same phrase again and again; other times I just play a few chords and let the sound slowly dissolve in the room.

This keyboard is in charge of making me stop.

The other keyboard sits in front of the computer.

With it I look for answers, write down ideas that appear out of nowhere, learn new things, and try to build the things in my head that did not exist before, one small piece at a time.

A web page, a piece of software, a stretch of writing — even an idea that turns up in the middle of the night and still seems interesting when I wake up — any of them can begin with a single keystroke.

Including the place you are looking at right now.

This keyboard is in charge of keeping me moving forward.

One is for playing, one is for creating.

One lets me find a moment of rest in reality; the other lets me slowly build the future I imagine.

And it is a strange coincidence that —

Tob was born on a keyboard in the first place.

Three letters left there casually, with no answer back then.

Years later, looking back, it is as if they found their own meaning.

## As for Miner

And I have grown fonder and fonder of its original, simple meaning.

A miner.

I love music, so I search among twelve notes for the sounds I like;

I love photography, so I search ordinary days for a moment worth keeping;

I love looking up at faraway places — stars, sky, and the contrails crossing the sky;

and I love technology: facing a question with no answer, then following one "why" after another, digging a little deeper each time.

Some questions can be answered in the end.

Some cannot.

And once in a while, somewhere in that aimless wandering, I bump into something that glimmers.

Maybe a harmony that suddenly sounds right, a photograph taken at exactly the right moment, a question that finally makes sense, or something that once existed only in my head and is now actually made.

Thinking about it that way, I suppose I really have been working as a "miner" all along.

It is just that at some point the pickaxe in my hand turned into —

piano keys, a lens, code, and an endless number of whys.

So I have never changed that accidentally given name.

MinerTob.

It was not created because it carried a meaning.

It was that, after keeping me company through the years,

it slowly grew a meaning of its own.

## About music

If I had to pick, out of all the things I love, the one that has stayed with me longest, it would probably still be music.

At first the keys were only keys.

Notes were only symbols on a score that had to be recognised.

As a child sitting at the piano, I did not think about what music actually meant; I only knew when I was supposed to press which key, and how to get a piece from its first bar to its last.

Only later did I gradually find that the same twelve notes can hold things far more complicated than language.

They can be bright, they can be melancholy, they can be calm, and they can surge.

Sometimes, changing a single note inside a single chord makes the whole world seem to change colour.

And a melody you truly love may not need any explanation at all.

By the time it sounds, it has already said everything.

## Why I like music

It has stopped before.

Things that were once familiar grow rusty, fingers forget movements that used to be effortless, and a piece played long ago may have to be picked up again.

But when I sat back down at the piano and gathered the scales, chords and phrases back one by one, I found that some things had not really left.

Fingers forget.

Ears and heart still remember.

And so music came back into my life.

Later I began to try other instruments, and to try writing down a few sounds of my own.

I love the strict, restrained order of classical music, and the simple, direct feeling of pop; the intricate structure of a complex work, and the moment of quiet that some ordinary chord can bring by accident.

I began to care more and more about the relationships between sounds.

Why should this note be here?

Why does the mood change completely when the chord changes?

Why do some melodies sound like something you have known somewhere, the very first time you hear them?

These questions do not all have answers.

But looking for the answers is already interesting enough.

## Understanding music

So for me, music has long meant more than "being able to play the piano".

It is more like a way of understanding the world, and of understanding myself.

Very often, when language does not know how to continue, music can finish the rest of the sentence for me.

When I am happy, I can play.

When I am sad, I can play.

When I do not want to express anything at all, I can play too.

I only have to sit down and let the first note sound.

And the world can be a little smaller for a while.

## The Rest Note

That is also why I eventually called this place:

The Rest Note.

Because music is not only sound.

Rests are part of the music too.

A rest looks like nothing at all.

No melody, no harmony, not even a sound.

But if you took every rest out of the music, the notes that remained would be crowded together with nowhere left to breathe.

So a rest is never where the music ends.

It only lets the sentence before it fall quiet,

lets the player breathe,

and gives the next melody room to begin again.

## Why this site exists

I want this site to be a place like that.

The internet is always fast.

Every day brings new messages, new arguments, new content, and everything is hurrying you to keep scrolling.

But here, there is no need to rush.

You can read an article.

Listen to music for a while.

Touch the piano keys a few times.

See what strange little thing I have been tinkering with.

Or do nothing at all, and just let the music keep playing.

I prefer to imagine this place as a small café tucked into a corner of the internet.

The door is not locked.

The music is already playing.

Now and then I will come back to change the record, put a new photograph on the wall, write a new article, or move in something I have just finished making.

Someone passes by, pushes the door open and sits for a while.

Then goes on their way.

That is enough.

Sit for a while, before the next bar begins.

## A few things that have not happened yet

There are still many things I want to do that have not happened yet.

So this part — rather than a "life plan", I prefer to call it:

a few things that have not happened yet.

Because a plan always sounds like a timetable that has to be followed exactly.

The future is not.

The future is more like a score that has not been finished yet.

Some places already have the outline of a melody; some have only a few vague chords; and there are wide, wide stretches of blank space.

As for what will actually happen when it is performed —

the me of right now does not know.

## What I hope for

I want to get into NAFA.

To keep studying music seriously there — music that has already been with me for many years.

I hope I can walk through the planned 3+2 step by step.

Not because the number "five years" means anything special in itself, but because I want to know:

if a person really is willing to keep going with the thing they have loved for years,

where can they finally get to?

This road probably will not be as easy as writing these few sentences.

There will be exams, practice repetitive enough to drive me up the wall, phrases I can never get right, and there will certainly be times when I doubt myself.

Some day I might even hate music for a while.

But I still hope I can keep going.

Not to prove anything.

I just hope that when I sit down at the piano again many years from now, I will still feel —

thank goodness I did not throw away the thing I loved.

## I also want to see Iceland once

To be exact,

I want to go to Iceland with my parents and my sister.

I have seen a lot of photographs of the aurora.

Green light across a black sky — sometimes only a faint arc, sometimes like the whole night sky slowly flowing.

The photographs are already beautiful enough.

But I still want to see it with my own eyes.

Not through a screen, and not through someone else's lens.

But really, after a long flight, in thick clothes, waiting for the dark somewhere far from city lights.

Maybe that day will be cold.

Maybe nothing will appear for a long time.

Maybe, when it finally does, it will not even be as dramatic as in the photos.

It does not matter.

Because I have imagined that scene many times:

when the aurora finally appears in the sky and I look up,

my parents and my sister happen to be standing right beside me.

Later I came to feel that the truly precious part of some scenery is not only how spectacular it is.

It is that years later, when you think of that sky again,

you still remember —

who you were watching it with that day.

## Later wishes become more ordinary instead

I want to have a small home of my own.

It does not have to be big.

Enough for a family of three is good.

Somewhere to eat together, a small space for each person, and ideally a window that afternoon sunlight can come through.

And of course,

a place has to be left for the piano.

Some sheet music will probably pile up beside it.

Books, a camera and small things brought back from trips go on the shelves.

The computer desk will probably still be a little messy.

And once in a while something will turn up that I bought because I found it fascinating, while everyone else has no idea what it is for.

If a bit of sky happens to be visible outside the window, even better.

When work is done, I can come back here.

Play the piano, write something, do a few things I enjoy.

Travel once in a while.

Take the camera out once in a while.

Go to the airport to watch planes once in a while.

On nights with good weather, maybe look up and hunt for stars.

And then, on an evening as ordinary as an evening can be,

a family back under the same light.

## I used to think an "ideal" had to be something grand

Later I kept feeling

that the future I actually want does not seem all that complicated.

To keep doing the things I love.

To make a living on my own.

To go and see the world I have always wanted to see.

To protect a little time that belongs to me.

To have people who matter beside me.

And finally, a place I know I can push the door open and walk into, no matter where I am coming back from.

That is probably already very good.

## Of course, none of this has happened yet

NAFA is still in the future.

That 3+2 score has not started playing.

The night sky over Iceland still exists only in the photographs I have seen.

And the small apartment that can hold a piano and a family of three — there is no telling which street it will finally appear on.

They may not even happen the way I imagine them today.

Life may modulate.

It may suddenly speed up, or have to slow down.

Some bars that were already written will be crossed out;

and some melody that was never planned may suddenly come in.

But I still want to leave these wishes here.

Because The Rest Note does not only record what has already happened.

It can also keep, for the me of today,

a little imagination about the future.

## Maybe, many years from now, I will open this page again

By then, some of these sentences should already be out of date.

If I really do get into NAFA,

I will come back and change:

"I want to get into NAFA."

to:

"I got into NAFA."

If I really do finish that 3+2,

I will leave one line after it:

"Done."

If I finally stand under the night sky in Iceland with my parents and my sister, and see the aurora I have waited so long for,

I hope I can come back and put up a photograph I took myself.

And then change:

"I want to go to Iceland with my parents and my sister."

to:

"We went together."

As for that small house —

if one day I really find it,

maybe I will sit beside the piano that finally has a place, open the computer, and come to this page once more.

By then, I do not know whether I will still like the words I am writing today.

I might find them a little childish.

I might laugh at how simply I thought about things back then.

I might also find that what I actually got is completely different from what I imagined.

But I do not think I will delete this page.

Because what it records is not someone who already knows how the story ends.

It is someone standing in front of the story,

who does not know the answer yet, but has already started walking.

## One last thing

If life really is a very long piece of music,

I still do not know which chord it will finally stop on.

But at least

I have written down some places I want to go,

met some sounds I love,

and I know roughly where the next melody wants to go.

The bars that have not been written down yet —

let us leave them to the future.

After all —

a rest is not the end.

After the rest, there is still a long melody.
